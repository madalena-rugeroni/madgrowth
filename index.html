The real problem

Years of credibility. No clear idea what to sell.

You've shipped products, led teams and hit the number at companies people have heard of. You want more flexibility, more autonomy — something of your own. But the moment you try to turn that into a business, the same four questions stop you.

01

What do I sell?

Ten years of experience contains a dozen possible businesses. Picking one feels like closing every other door.

02

Who is it for?

"I help companies grow" isn't positioning. Without a sharp who and a reason it's you, nobody knows when to call you.

03

How do I package it?

Hours, a retainer, an audit, a product? The same expertise sells very differently depending on the shape you give it.

04

What do I charge?

Most operators anchor on their old salary, price too low, and end up working twice as hard for half the upside.

How it works

Four questions, answered in order — then launched

Something of your own isn't a leap. It's a sequence — the same one I used, now encoded into tools you can run.

01

Find your angle

What do I sell?

Your career already contains the business. The free Expertise Audit finds your three strongest monetizable angles.

02

Position it

Who is it for?

One person, one problem, one promise. Four skills: talk to customers, define who it's for, position it, put it into words — every claim graded on evidence, not hope.

03

Package it

How do I package it? What do I charge?

An offer with a price — not a someday idea. Three skills: the offer, the pricing, the numbers. Most people get this wrong by pricing too low and promising too vaguely.

04

Build the audience path

Who hears about it?

Two skills: a content engine built from your own message, and one lead magnet that bridges to your offer. Inbound without 100k followers.

05

Launch it

Who pays first?

Five skills: the page, the launch sequence, the get-customers plan, the conversion path, the first 90 days. Direct outreach to your first ten prospects included — a launch is a sales motion, not a waiting room.

Why not just…

You've probably tried some of these already

…ask ChatGPT or Claude?

You'll get a plausible answer to every question and a decision on none. The Stack is a system: it asks about your business once, loads the playbook for your kind of business, and every skill builds on the last.

…take a creator course?

Most teach you to build an audience first. Senior operators need the opposite order: an offer and a price, then the smallest audience that buys it.

…join a fractional marketplace?

Useful once you know exactly what you sell and to whom. Marketplaces place people who can already pitch themselves — and keep a cut of every engagement.

…figure it out alone?

Possible. It's also why most people think about this for months — sometimes years — without launching anything.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do I need to quit my job?

No. The system is designed to run alongside a career. Most clients build their first offer while employed.

What if I don't know what I'd sell?

That's step one, and it's where most people are. The free Expertise Audit skill finds your three strongest angles in about ten minutes — from the career you already have, not a new idea.

I already use ChatGPT or Claude. Why pay for skills?

A prompt answers a question; a system makes a decision. The first skill asks eight questions about your business once. Every other skill reads those answers, loads the playbook for your kind of business, and ends with an artifact — an ICP, an offer, a price, a launch plan — not a chat transcript you have to interpret.

Fractional, services or software — does it matter which?

Not to start. The first steps are the same for all three: what you sell, who it's for, what it costs. The skills route to a different playbook for each, and the go / no-go scores the idea against your Builder Archetype before you commit months to it.

I have around 5 years of experience. Is that enough?

If you've owned an outcome — a number, a team, a launch — yes. The Expertise Audit looks for the angle in what you've actually done, not in your title.

Do I need a big audience?

No. The audience step builds inbound from a small, right-sized presence. A well-executed launch beats ten thousand followers.
