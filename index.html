Where it stalls

Most leaders in tech know roughly what they want to build. Then it sits for months.

You've shipped products, led teams and hit targets at companies people know. You want more control over your time and something of your own. Maybe that's a fractional role, an advisory practice, an agency or a SaaS idea you keep coming back to. Four questions keep it from starting.

01

Is this the right thing to build?

You have an idea, maybe three. With no way to test them against the market and against how you like to work, all of them stay open and none of them start.

02

Who buys it, and why me?

"I help companies grow" gives nobody a reason to call. A buyer has to recognize their problem in one line and see why you're the person to fix it.

03

How do I package and price it?

Day rate, retainer, project or product. Most people anchor on their last salary, charge too little and end up with more work than they planned.

04

Where do the first clients come from?

Your network gets you a few conversations. After that you need people who already know what you do before you pitch them, and that audience takes months to build.

How it works

Five steps from an idea to paying clients

It's the sequence I used to build my own businesses. Every product below follows these five steps. The difference is who does the work.

01

Decide

Is this worth building?

The Go / No-Go scores your idea against the market and your Builder Archetype, then gives you the cheapest way to test it. No idea yet? Start with the free Expertise Audit.

02

Position it

Who buys it, and why me?

Customer interviews, your ICP, positioning and messaging. Every claim gets an evidence grade, so you know what's proven and what's still a guess.

03

Package it

What do I sell, and for how much?

The offer, the price and the numbers behind it. You end with a clear scope, a price you can defend and a check that the economics work.

04

Build the audience

Who hears about it?

A content plan built from your messaging and one lead magnet that leads to your offer. Start this early, because inbound takes months to build.

05

Launch it

Who pays first?

The landing page, the launch plan, your acquisition channels, the sales conversation and a client's first 90 days.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do I need to quit my job?

No. The system is built to run alongside a job, and most clients create their first offer while still employed.

What if I don't know what I'd sell?

Many people have a direction and no specific offer yet. The free Expertise Audit finds your three strongest angles in about ten minutes, using the career you already have, and the Go / No-Go tells you which one is worth building.

I already use ChatGPT or Claude. Why pay for The Stack?

A general assistant gives you a reasonable answer for any business. The Stack runs a specific agent for the business you're building, based on my own go-to-market work and on frameworks from practitioners like April Dunford and Blair Enns. Each agent uses what the earlier ones produced and ends with a document you keep: an ICP, an offer, a price, a launch plan.

Fractional, services or software: does it matter which?

Not at the start. All of them begin with what you sell, who buys it and what it costs. The Stack has a version of each agent for each model, and in The Build we adapt the plan to yours. The Go / No-Go scores the idea against your Builder Archetype before you commit months to it.

I have around 5 years of experience. Is that enough?

If you've owned an outcome, like a number, a team or a launch, yes. The Expertise Audit looks for the angle in the work you've done, whatever your title was.

Do I need a big audience?

Not a big one. You do need a focused one, and it pays to start early. The audience step builds it from a content plan and one lead magnet, and a few hundred of the right people is enough to launch.

How do I go from a tech job to fractional or consulting work?

Start with a problem you've solved more than once and a type of company that has that problem and the budget to fix it. Package it as a monthly retainer with a clear scope and sell it to your network first. The Builder Diagnostic shows whether fractional work suits how you operate, and The Stack takes you through positioning, the offer and pricing.

How should I price a fractional role or consulting offer?

Price against the cost of the problem and the full-time hire the client would otherwise make, and sell a defined monthly scope in place of hours. The Pricing skill in The Stack runs this with Blair Enns and Alan Weiss for services and Madhavan Ramanujam for software.
